# The first two seconds

Everything else in a short is downstream of the opening. A weak hook does not
make a video perform slightly worse — it makes the rest of the video
irrelevant, because almost nobody sees it.

So the hook is written first, approved first, and everything else is built to
serve it.

## What a hook has to do

Create an open question the viewer needs closed. That is the whole mechanism.
"This is interesting" does not hold anyone; "wait, is that true?" does.

Three things have to happen at once, in about two seconds:

1. **The voice says something that opens a loop**
2. **The screen shows text that stands on its own** — sound is off
3. **Something moves**, so the frame does not read as a paused video

## Patterns that work

**Name the mistake.** "You're pricing your work wrong, and it's costing you
clients." Specific, immediately about them.

**Promise one outcome.** "Three settings, and your videos stop looking
amateur." One outcome, not four benefits.

**Start mid-story.** "So the client rejected it twice, and then..." The
viewer has to stay to find out what they walked in on.

**Contradict what they believe.** "Posting more is why your account stopped
growing."

**Hard number.** "Eighty percent of people watch this with the sound off."
Numbers are concrete in a way adjectives never are.

**Question they cannot answer.** "Can you tell which of these was made by
AI?" — only if you actually pay it off.

## What kills it

**Introducing yourself.** "Hey guys, welcome back" — the fastest exit in
short-form. They do not know you yet and they did not ask.

**Announcing the video.** "In this video I'm going to explain..." Just
explain it. The announcement is two seconds of nothing.

**Warming up.** "So, a lot of people have been asking me about..." Cut every
word before the first interesting one.

**Context first.** Background belongs after the hook, not before it. Start
with the interesting part and backfill.

**A hook the video does not pay off.** It works once and costs you the next
video. Clickbait trains people to leave.

## Structure after the hook

A 30-60 second short usually goes:

| Part | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-3s | open the loop |
| Context | 3-8s | just enough to make the payoff land |
| Payoff | 8-40s | the actual content, the promise kept |
| Close | last 3-5s | one action, or a reason to watch again |

Keep the close short. A long outro is watch time spent on nothing, and watch
time is the thing you are optimizing.

## One call to action

One. "Follow, comment, share, and check the link" gets none of them. Pick the
single thing that matters and ask for it plainly.

## Testing the hook without making the video

Say the first line out loud to someone who does not know the topic. If they
ask a follow-up question, the loop is open. If they say "okay", rewrite it.

That takes fifteen seconds and saves the hour you would spend animating a
video nobody watches past the first line.
